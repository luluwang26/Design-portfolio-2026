Fraud risk and customer trust: “Let me in”

Effective identity verification for employees of existing accounts.

At a glance
Summary:
Employees of companies already on our network were blocked from getting paid because they couldn't log in to their company's existing account, so many created duplicates instead. I mapped where the system could step in, took 3 options to Risk and Compliance, and designed the one we could ship with minimal engineering: recognize the employee at the duplicate check, release their first payment, and let the account's admins approve them as a member.
Design process:
Legacy experience audit → Listen to Gong calls on customer complaints → Redesign onboarding flow for this user group → Consult Risk and Compliance team → Iteration & final design
Team:
1 product manager, 1 product designer, 1 Risk consultant, 1 Compliance consultant, 2 engineers.
Outcome:
Experiment running against legacy; results pending.
Key trade-off:
The question changed from “how do we verify everyone?” to “how much risk on one payment is worth the trust of tens of thousands of users a month?”

01Context & problem

When an employee of a company who already has an account with us tries to sign up a new account to get paid, we block the creation of the duplicate account and ask the employee to log in with their company’s existing account. Often times, this employee doesn’t have access to the log in credentials so they can’t get paid, causing customer complaints.

The creation of new Basic Receivable (BR) organizations for existing Virtual Card (VC) suppliers leads to:

  • Network Inefficiency: Duplicate organizations create a “noisy network,” which degrades the effectiveness of autoconnection and search, confusing users while damaging vendor data integrity.
  • Revenue Leakage: For existing VC suppliers, duplicate BRs cause significant Total Payment Volume (TPV) leakage and introduce reconciliation and trust risks.
  • User Urgency vs. Access: Users often opt to create a new account instead of claiming an existing one because their primary goal is to get paid quickly, but they are unable to immediately log in to the system-identified existing organization.

A desired outcome is to quickly verify the employee’s identity and relationship with the existing VC account while keeping bad actors out, and safely add them as a member to the account.

02When & how should we intervene?

I started by auditing the existing flow. Here are some concepts that are good to know:

  • Payment can only be sent between two connected nodes in the payment networks.
  • Each node can accept payment via a bank account for ACH, a physical address for check, or an email address for Virtual Card remit info.
  • Each node has a Payment Network ID (PNI) which is the most accurate identifier of a node in the network.
  • Vendor data is an ever changing creature, bank account, address, email can be changed but not updated in our network, therefore they can not be used as a source-of-truth.
  • Ways to validate a vendor indeed belongs to a company include (but are not limited to): Bank account matching, invoice matching, PNI matching, account mask validation.
  • If the BR signs up from the marketing site directly, we can’t trace who their payor is so their newly created BR account is not connected to the AP hence no payment flow would happen. If the BR signs up from the AP’s invite email then they’ll be auto-connected upon acceptance.

The team brainstormed dozens of ways to allow the BR obtain the VC remit info without moving mountains of the existing pipeline, and 3 options stood out.

03Risk Triage

The PM and I showed our 3 options to the company’s Risk team and Compliance team for feedback. We were told the Compliance team was not concerned as no Personally Identifiable Information (PII) was at risk.

However, Risk flagged potential risk of losing the first payment if the BR is fraudulent. The decision fell back on our product team to balance the risk we are comfortable to take to gain the trust from over tens of thousands of users each month.

We ended up choosing option 2 to run as an experiment against legacy, while exploring external resource on a vendor identity verification solution.

04Design concepts

Here are the changes we made in the new design:

  • New content for the 2nd CTA informing BR that their payment is here.
  • A confirmation step with clear explanation on next steps is added.
  • New payment widget leading BR to their payment.

On the back-end, the Eng team also need to implement:

  • Check business relationship once a duplicate account is detected, based on result dynamically show the content for the 2nd CTA on De-Dup step.
  • Route the latest pending payment to the VC org to this BR org.
  • Send email informing admins of existing VC org about the payment and ask for their approval.

This solution gives the employees of an existing VC account a way to receive their 1st payment while they verify themselves. The admins of the existing account will receive an email informing them a new member requests to join, and decide to accept or reject. In general, the risk is worth the better user experience for this unique user group.

05What I’ve learned

  1. Ask what you're actually protecting. The legacy gate guarded an account login, but the real exposure was a single first payment. Once we named that, the question changed from “how do we verify everyone?” to “how much risk on one payment is worth the trust of tens of thousands of users a month?”
  2. Risk is a product decision. Risk and Compliance gave us the facts: no PII exposed, but possible loss of a first payment. The call on what risk to accept was ours. Bringing 3 options with the trade-offs laid out (fraud risk, engineering effort, user experience) turned a review into a decision.
  3. Ship the smallest change that tests the bet, and keep the search on for a solution. Option 2 was new wording and one confirmation step on an existing screen, so we could learn from real users while the bigger fix is explored. The existing account's admins approve new members, because they know who works there; the system doesn't have to guess.
